November 17, 2026 Release

v1.3.0: Meetings, Failure Notifications and voxtype doctor

voxtype 1.3.0 is the meetings release. A meeting streams its transcript as it records, to a live view on your desktop and to a socket other programs can read. Your lines and the other side's interleave in the order they were spoken, and speaker echo stays out of your lines. After the call you can fix any line, search every meeting, get a summary from your own coding agent or a local model, send the action items to Todoist, and share the notes.

It's also the release where voxtype tells you when something fails. Carmine Paolino wrote fifteen pull requests that turn failures which only reached the log into notifications that name the fix, and added voxtype doctor.

Meeting mode stays off on existing installs until you turn it on. The new notifications are on for everyone, so read Upgrading before you install. Everything in 1.2.2 is included.

The live meeting view (#809)

voxtype meeting view opens a card at the right edge of the screen that follows the meeting as it records. It opens by itself when a meeting starts, and it never takes keyboard focus from your call.

  • Transcript: one line per utterance with its time and speaker. Click a speaker to name them, or the speaker on one line to move only that line. Hover a line to play the audio from it, edit its text, add a note or highlight, or delete it. Edits, moves and deletes offer Undo for a few seconds.
  • Notes: your own notes for the meeting, saved as you type.
  • Ask: questions about what has been said so far. Times in answers jump to the line.
  • Summary: overview, decisions, action items and chapters once the meeting stops, with Regenerate after you fix speakers or lines.

The header has Pause, Resume and Stop, live level meters for your mic and the call, and a menu that exports Markdown, text, JSON, SRT or WebVTT into ~/Documents/Voxtype/. With no meeting running, the view lists past meetings and searches them. An ended meeting with kept audio gets a waveform you can click to play from. The card can be moved, resized, made see-through and minimized to a pill that shows the state, the time and the last line.

It runs in the Omarchy shell as part of the Voxtype panel, and in voxtype's own Quickshell shell elsewhere:

voxtype setup quickshell
voxtype meeting view latest --tab summary
config.toml
[meeting]
show_view_on_start = true
view_opacity = 0.8
status_notifications = "auto"   # skip Started/Ended notices while the view is showing

Lines arrive once per chunk (chunk_duration_secs, 30 seconds by default), not word by word. Requested by @sintel-be.

Why use it: you see who said what during the call and fix it right after, without a terminal.

No Quickshell? A window, or your terminal

On GNOME, KDE Plasma, Sway, X11 and other desktops without Quickshell, a meeting opens in an ordinary GTK 4 window, voxtype-meetings-gtk4. It has the transcript with level meters, Pause and Stop, speaker renaming, Move line and Note on line, your notes, Ask and the summary. Past meetings opened from its list are read-only there.

voxtype meeting watch shows the same meeting in a terminal, on any desktop and over SSH, with keys to pause, stop, move a line, add a note and ask.

config.toml
[meeting]
view = "auto"   # auto | quickshell | gtk4 | terminal | none

auto picks the Quickshell view when Quickshell can run, then the GTK 4 window when it is installed and GTK 4 loads. terminal is never picked by auto.

The window ships in the release .deb and .rpm. The source RPM and Debian packages build it as a voxtype-meetings-gtk4 subpackage that the main package recommends, so a from-source install (COPR, a Debian rebuild) gets the same window. On NixOS it's the flake's meetings-gtk4 output.

Why use it: meetings are no longer tied to one desktop shell.

Meetings start at once, and can use their own engine (#808, #717)

A meeting used to load a second copy of the transcription model, even when dictation already had it loaded. It now uses dictation's loaded model when the engine, model and language match. Dictation keeps working during the meeting, and the two take turns on the model. Measured from the start command to the meeting being ready to transcribe:

Engine1.2.21.3.0
Parakeet Ultra Q8 (GGUF worker)0.30 s0.005 s
Cohere q4 (ONNX)0.84 s0.004 s
Whisper large-v3-turbo0.42 s0.003 s

Recording starts before any model loads, so the first sentence of a meeting is no longer lost. A meeting can also use its own engine, model or language, and keep a link to the call:

voxtype meeting start --model large-v3-turbo --language de
voxtype meeting start --engine parakeet --model parakeet-ultra-Q8_0.gguf
voxtype meeting start --title "Planning" --url https://meet.example.com/abc-defg-hij

VOXTYPE_MEETING_ENGINE, VOXTYPE_MEETING_MODEL and VOXTYPE_MEETING_LANGUAGE do the same. A choice the meeting can't use is refused before it starts, with the command that fixes it. Reported by @yaubara and @thrawny.

Speaker echo stays out of your lines

On speakers, your mic hears the other side of the call, and that used to be transcribed as You.

Echo cancellation. While a meeting records on speakers, voxtype runs PipeWire's WebRTC echo canceller on the meeting's mic, using what your speakers play as the reference. It starts and stops with the meeting and changes nothing in your PipeWire configuration. With headphones, a headset or Bluetooth there's no echo to cancel, and GTCRN runs as before.

config.toml
[meeting.audio]
echo_cancel = "auto"   # auto | webrtc | gtcrn | disabled

Echo guard. For every mic line, voxtype compares the two tracks' levels. A line the loopback explains is dropped when the remote track has those words, and moved to the remote speaker when it doesn't. Short misheard echoes, the "Thank you." a speech engine writes for noise, and a decoder repeating one phrase six times are dropped or collapsed. A line with any of your own voice in it stays yours.

config.toml
[meeting.audio]
echo_guard = true
echo_fuzzy_max_words = 4

voxtype meeting status --json, each meeting's meeting.json and the meeting socket say which path a meeting used: webrtc, gtcrn or none.

Why use it: you can take calls on speakers and still get a transcript where You means you.

Lines in speaking order, for every engine

Each line is timed per utterance, so the two sides interleave as they were spoken instead of a chunk at a time. Parakeet uses its own word times, in-process and through the GGUF worker. Whisper gets a line per utterance from one call per chunk, whisper-cli a line per segment, and remote Whisper uses the server's segment times. Chunks end in a pause both tracks share, so fewer sentences are split across two chunks.

Hybrid diarization: You on the mic, remote voices told apart

The hybrid backend keeps everything on your mic as You and clusters only the system audio. One remote voice stays Remote. When a second voice speaks they become Remote 1 and Remote 2, and earlier lines are renamed. Short utterances no longer split one caller into several speakers.

voxtype config set meeting.diarization.backend hybrid

Hybrid needs the ONNX builds and the ECAPA model that ml uses. Without them it falls back to simple and a notification says why.

Fix the transcript, during the call or after

voxtype meeting label latest "Remote 1" Dana
voxtype meeting reassign latest 28 Stephanie     # --reset undoes it
voxtype meeting edit latest 28 "fixed text"      # --reset restores the original
voxtype meeting delete-line latest 31            # --restore brings it back
voxtype meeting annotate latest 28 "check this number" --highlight
voxtype meeting notes latest --append "follow up with Steve"
voxtype meeting title latest "Pete <> Chad Bi-Weekly"
  • label names a speaker and survives the daemon's saves during a meeting. If the meeting has a summary, action items owned by the old name take the new one. Only exact matches change, so naming Chris never touches Christopher.
  • reassign moves one line, edit corrects its text, delete-line hides it. The originals are kept, and every change can be undone.
  • annotate attaches a note or a highlight to a line, and notes keeps free-text notes in the meeting's notes.md.
  • title renames a meeting at any time, live or finished.
  • Each meeting folder gets a transcript.md with notes at the top and annotations under their lines.

Your name, and names from the title

Set your name once and it replaces "You" everywhere voxtype shows your side: the views, exports, summaries, the assistant and search.

config.toml
[meeting]
my_name = "Pete"

A two-person title names both sides on its own. In "Pete <> Andrew standup", your side is Pete and every remote voice is Andrew, so a 1:1 never shows three Remotes. A name you gave by hand always wins.

config.toml
[meeting]
auto_label_from_title = true

Titles from your calendar

A meeting that starts without a title you chose takes its name, and its call link, from the calendar event in progress. Voxtype asks your calendar's private iCal link first, then a calendar command, then the Omarchy Next Meeting widget when it's on your bar.

voxtype meeting calendar set-ics -        # reads the private iCal link from stdin, kept outside config.toml
config.toml
[meeting.calendar]
window_mins = 10

Search every meeting

voxtype meeting search budget
voxtype meeting search budget review --speaker Dana
voxtype meeting search '"next quarter"' --since 2026-11-01 --json

Search covers transcripts as you see them (names, moves and corrections applied, deleted lines left out), plus titles, notes and summaries. Meetings recorded before this release are indexed the first time you search.

Ask about a meeting, and get a summary when it ends

voxtype meeting ask answers questions about a meeting, and a summary is written when it stops. Both go through your own coding agent, run headless with every tool turned off, or through a local Ollama model.

voxtype meeting assistant allow claude
voxtype meeting ask latest "what did Dana just say?" --last 5m
voxtype meeting summarize latest --regenerate
  • Supported agents are claude, pi and codex. Codex runs inside a bubblewrap jail that sees nothing from your home directory except its own auth.json, and needs bwrap.
  • No transcript goes to an agent until you allow that agent once. local_only = true sends nothing to an agent.
  • The prompt carries your speaker names, moves, notes and annotations as you see them.
config.toml
[meeting.assistant]
agent = "claude"      # unset: the Omarchy default agent, then claude or pi on PATH
local_only = false

[meeting.summary]
auto = true           # unset: on once an agent has been allowed

Action items to Todoist or a reminder

The summary's action items carry an owner, the deadline when someone said one, and the moment in the meeting it came up.

voxtype meeting actions latest                           # list them
voxtype meeting actions latest --mine                    # only yours
voxtype meeting todoist connect                          # once, in your browser
voxtype meeting actions latest --send todoist            # every item, or --pick 1,3
voxtype meeting actions latest --send reminder --in 30   # Omarchy reminders

"Friday" in a Thursday meeting becomes the next day. Each send is remembered, so sending again never makes a second task. The Summary tab has the same items as rows with Send to Todoist and Remind me.

todoist connect signs voxtype in through Todoist's consent page and asks only to add tasks. It can't read your tasks or projects. The token is kept in ~/.local/state/voxtype/todoist.json, never in config.toml. On a machine without a browser, point [meeting.actions] todoist_token_file at a file holding a personal API token.

Why use it: the follow-ups from a call end up where you already track work.

Share a meeting

voxtype meeting share latest                    # copy the notes to the clipboard
voxtype meeting share latest --transcript       # ...with the transcript
voxtype meeting share latest --to email         # open a mail draft
voxtype meeting share latest --to file          # save to ~/Documents/Voxtype
voxtype meeting share latest --to command       # pipe to [meeting.share] command
voxtype meeting share latest --preview          # print what would be sent

A share has the title, date, attendees, summary, action items, your notes and highlighted lines. Nothing is shared unless you run the command or pick the menu item, and there is no hosted link.

Keep the audio, check it first, play it back (#529)

retain_audio = true keeps a meeting's audio as mic.opus, loopback.opus and mixed.opus in its folder, or as WAV files when ffmpeg isn't installed. In 1.2.2 the setting did nothing. Reported by @loicfavory.

voxtype meeting soundcheck                  # meters for mic and call before you join
voxtype meeting play latest --segment 28
voxtype meeting play latest --at 12:40
voxtype meeting play --stop

Playback needs ffmpeg and one of pw-play, paplay or aplay. Each meeting folder also has a meeting.json describing the meeting and its files, and post_meeting_command runs once the folder is final:

config.toml
[meeting]
retain_audio = true
post_meeting_command = 'rsync -a "$VOXTYPE_MEETING_DIR" nas:meetings/'

A crash no longer costs you the meeting

The transcript is saved after every chunk. If the daemon dies mid-meeting, the next start marks the meeting interrupted, and the view and a notification offer to finish it: transcribe the audio left over, write the summary and run post_meeting_command.

voxtype meeting recover
voxtype meeting recover --finish 3f2a
voxtype meeting recover --discard 3f2a

Import recordings, and transcribe meetings again (#489)

voxtype meeting import call.mp3 --title "Client call"
voxtype meeting import recording.wav --channels mic,loopback
voxtype meeting retranscribe latest --model large-v3-turbo

import turns any file ffmpeg reads into a meeting. --channels mic,loopback attributes a two-channel call recording to You and Remote. retranscribe runs a meeting's kept audio through another engine, model or language, keeps the old transcript as transcript.v1.json, and carries your annotations, moves and corrections over. Requested by @fidalgo.

Voxtype offers to record a call, and stops when it ends

When a call app starts using the mic, voxtype shows one notification offering to record it. Zoom and teams-for-linux count on that alone. A browser counts only when its Hyprland window title confirms a call, such as Google Meet or Teams on the web. Nothing records until you press Record.

A browser call goes on while the browser still has the microphone or a window still shows it. Bringing another tab to the front doesn't end it. When the call ends, voxtype says so and stops the meeting 20 seconds later unless you press Keep recording.

config.toml
[meeting.detect]
enabled = true            # unset: on for Omarchy, off elsewhere
auto_record_apps = []     # e.g. ["Zoom"] to record without asking
on_call_end = "stop"      # stop | ask | keep
stop_grace_secs = 20

voxtype meeting suggest --dry-run --watch prints what the detector sees and why.

Tell the call it's being recorded (off by default)

Laws on recording calls differ by place, and some need everyone's agreement. Until you acknowledge it, meeting start and the meeting view remind you to tell the other people (voxtype meeting notice --ack).

Voxtype can also say it for you. With the announcement on, "This meeting is being recorded and transcribed with Voxtype." plays once when the meeting starts. It goes into the call app's own mic stream, so the other people hear it even when you wear headphones, and it plays on your speakers too. It stays out of the transcript.

Call apps open the mic on their pre-join screen and again when you join. The announcement waits until the call app has kept the same microphone stream for five seconds, so it plays to the meeting and not to the lobby.

config.toml
[meeting]
announce_recording = false                  # true to announce
announcement_voice = "kokoro"               # or "jessica", downloaded on first use
# announcement_file = "~/announce-de.wav"   # your own wording or language
voxtype meeting start --announce
voxtype meeting start --no-announce

meeting status and the view say whether it reached the call or played only on your speakers, and why. It needs PipeWire. It's a courtesy, not consent: it doesn't ask anyone anything, and a muted mic in the call app silences it. You remain responsible for the recording laws that apply to you.

The built-in voice is AI-generated speech from Kokoro-82M (voice af_heart). It ships in every build, plays offline, and is MIT like the rest of voxtype. Voxtype never calls a speech service.

You can pick an ElevenLabs voice, Jessica, instead. It isn't part of voxtype or any package: voxtype downloads it once from its models server, checks it against its published sha256 and keeps it in ~/.local/share/voxtype/sounds/. Its terms aren't MIT: it may be redistributed only unmodified, with voxtype, and may not be used to train or improve AI models. Voxtype shows them when it downloads.

voxtype meeting announcement --voice jessica              # download it now
voxtype config set meeting.announcement_voice jessica

If a meeting wants Jessica and can't download it within 15 seconds, the built-in voice plays, the meeting goes on, and meeting status says why.

Meeting settings in the terminal

The Meeting page of voxtype configure now lists every [meeting] setting, grouped the way the Settings panel groups them (General, Audio, Speakers, Calendar, Summary, Action items, Sharing, Call detection, Assistant), with the same labels and help. Before, it had three hand-written fields, and one of them wrote a key nothing read. Changes are saved in one go, comments in your config are kept, and the page says which settings need a daemon restart.

Why use it: you can set up meetings over SSH or without a desktop panel.

Failures show a notification that names the fix (#880 to #896)

Before 1.3.0, most failures only reached the journal. You pressed the hotkey and nothing happened. Each of these now shows a desktop notification that says what failed and what to run:

  • The text couldn't be typed. "Voxtype: copied to clipboard, paste manually" lists each typing tool that was skipped and why. If nothing could deliver the text at all, "Voxtype: transcription not delivered" shows the text and saves it (#880).
  • The microphone or the model failed to load. The notice names the device or the model and the command that fixes it (#885).
  • The built-in hotkey can't open a keyboard. It shows once, and it names [hotkey] enabled = false for people who use a compositor keybinding (#884).
  • VAD can't start. With backend = "auto", voxtype now falls back to Energy VAD instead of running without any. With backend = "whisper" and no model, a notice at startup gives the fix (#889).
  • Whisper or OpenVINO fell back from the GPU or NPU to the CPU, or a CUDA or MIGraphX build can't find its GPU libraries (#895, #891).
  • The OSD frontend you set can't run and another one took over (#883).
  • A post-processing command or an output hook failed. The text is typed as transcribed, and the notice says what went wrong (#892).
  • A meeting started without the other side's audio, without speaker separation or without echo cancellation, or its transcription failed (#896).
  • A recording is about to hit max_duration_secs, and again when it does (#891).
  • A transcription failed. The notice gives the command that retries the recording (#891).

Notifications are delivered over D-Bus when notify-send is missing or fails, so they no longer depend on libnotify (#881).

Why use it: a hotkey that does nothing now comes with a reason and a command.

voxtype doctor (#887)

One command checks the config, the model, the GPU runtime, the audio input, the output tools, input access, notifications, the OSD, meeting tools and the service. Every problem comes with its fix on the next line:

Transcription:
  ✗ whisper model 'medium.en' is not downloaded
      Fix: Run: voxtype setup --download --model medium.en
voxtype doctor
voxtype doctor --json

It exits 1 when a check failed, and it only checks features your config turns on. The daemon runs the quick part of the same checks at startup and groups what it finds into one notification, "Voxtype: N problems need attention". voxtype setup check prints the same report under its old name.

Why use it: it's the first thing to run when voxtype doesn't work, and the first thing to paste into a bug report.

Failed recordings and undelivered text are kept (#891, #880)

A recording whose transcription fails is saved as a WAV in ~/.local/state/voxtype/failed-audio/. The 20 most recent are kept, and voxtype transcribe <file> retries one.

A transcription that could be neither typed nor copied is saved as a text file in ~/.local/state/voxtype/undelivered/. The 200 most recent are kept.

Both directories and their files are readable only by you. Both are on by default. Each has a setting:

config.toml
[audio]
keep_failed_recordings = false

[output]
keep_undelivered_text = false

Why use it: a failed dictation used to be gone. Now you can retry it or copy it out of a file.

Paste keys follow the window (#882)

ctrl+v doesn't paste in a terminal. With output.paste_keys = "auto", paste mode sends ctrl+v to GUI apps and output.paste_keys_terminal (default ctrl+shift+v) to terminals. It works on Hyprland, Sway and niri. Elsewhere auto sends ctrl+v.

config.toml
[output]
mode = "paste"
paste_keys = "auto"
paste_keys_terminal = "ctrl+shift+v"

auto is the default when the key is absent. An explicit paste_keys is sent everywhere, as before.

OSD: a fixed level scale, placement per output, opacity (#879, #894)

  • The GTK4 and native waveform and level meter use a fixed dB scale, so the height shows how loud the mic is. waveform_gain shifts that scale: 10 is neutral, and every doubling shows the input 6 dB louder.
  • Placement is computed from the focused output's own size, so monitors with different resolutions and scales get the same relative position.
  • [osd] opacity now applies in the GTK4 and native frontends.
  • The native OSD renders sharp at fractional scales, follows the theme colors and picks up an Omarchy theme switch without a restart.

voxtype setup lard downloads the cleanup model

The LARD disfluency model removes fillers and false starts from English dictation. It's on the model mirror now. Installing it is one command:

voxtype setup lard
voxtype config set text.lard_disfluency true
systemctl --user restart voxtype

The model is 64 KB, checked against a pinned sha256, and licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, separately from voxtype's MIT code (model card). Nothing downloads unless you run the command.

When text.lard_disfluency is on and the model is missing, voxtype doctor, the startup notification, voxtype config set, the Settings panel and voxtype configure say so and name the command.

Dictated text stays out of the log

At the default level the log records lengths, not words:

Transcribed 29 chars (whisper engine)

The same goes for meeting lines, undelivered text and text sent to a post-processing command. The systemd service logs to the journal, and the journal keeps what it's given for a long time. Run with -v to log the text when you report a recognition problem.

voxtype-git for Arch (#757)

packaging/arch-git/ is a source-package recipe that builds the development branch, with Whisper tuned for the machine that builds it, the Vulkan and ONNX variants, the GGUF workers and the desktop pieces. It isn't on AUR yet. Also by Carmine Paolino.

Models come only from Voxtype's mirror

Whisper models, the Silero VAD model, and the ECAPA and GTCRN models that meetings use now download from models.voxtype.io, like every other model voxtype downloads. Each file is checked against the mirror's manifest, size and sha256, before it takes the name voxtype loads, so a damaged or swapped download never runs. There's no Hugging Face or GitHub fallback any more. Files already on disk keep their names and aren't downloaded again when they match.

Meetings from the tray, and on for new installs

The tray has Start Meeting, then Stop, Pause and Resume while one runs, and opens the meeting view and the Voxtype panel. New installs get [meeting] enabled = true. Existing configs keep what they have, and while meeting mode is off the tray offers Turn on meetings....

The packages install a Voxtype Meetings launcher entry. The Nix flake now ships the meeting view, the GGUF worker and the desktop entries.

For scripts and status bars (#518)

  • $XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/voxtype/meeting.sock streams meeting events as newline-delimited JSON: a hello snapshot, then segments, status, levels, speaker_renamed, segment_updated, segment_deleted, title_changed, notes_updated, summary_ready, summary_updated, announcement, playback and import progress.
  • meeting status, show, list, search, actions, share --preview, ask, play, reassign, edit, delete-line, annotate, notes and title take --json.
  • voxtype status --format json --extended adds meeting_active and meeting_status, and meeting_id while a meeting runs.

The contracts are in INTEGRATIONS.md. Requested by @malko.

Bug fixes

  • A chunk holding one short sentence, such as an opening line while people join, was dropped. It is now transcribed
  • Segment IDs repeated across chunks. They are now unique within a meeting
  • With loopback_device = "auto", the loopback took the first running monitor, so on a card with several outputs it could record an idle one. It now records the default output's monitor. Thanks to @materemias, who proposed the same fix in #516
  • Speaker labels given during a meeting were lost when the next chunk was saved
  • meeting export --help listed three formats while five worked
  • Meeting notifications named the meeting by its UUID. They now use its title, and Ended says how long it ran
  • A timed-out post_meeting_command is killed before it's reaped
  • The OSD's and the daemon's theme watcher could abort the whole process when its file-watching loop hit an error. A dead watcher is now replaced, and the theme is reloaded only if it changed meanwhile
  • A signal arriving at the wrong moment could turn an interrupted system call into a "Resource temporarily unavailable" error, behind the theme watcher failure above and spurious remote-transcription timeouts. signal-hook-registry 1.4.8 preserves errno
  • With [vad] backend = "auto", the Whisper engine and no Silero model, the daemon ran with no VAD at all. It now uses Energy VAD
  • The native OSD drew a washed-out grey background and came out blurry on scaled outputs

Every voxtype notification is sent under the app name Voxtype, so a rule in your notification daemon can mute them all.

  • If notify-send isn't installed, 1.2.2 showed you no notifications. 1.3.0 delivers them over D-Bus, so you'll now see all of them, including the one after each dictation. [output.notification] on_transcription = false turns that one off.
  • A working setup should stay quiet. If you get a notice for something that works, please report it.

Dictation

  • Failed recordings and undelivered text are saved by default, in ~/.local/state/voxtype/failed-audio/ (20 files) and ~/.local/state/voxtype/undelivered/ (200 files), readable only by you. Set [audio] keep_failed_recordings = false and [output] keep_undelivered_text = false to write nothing. Files saved earlier stay until you delete them.
  • The log no longer has your words. Scripts that grep the journal for Transcribed: "..." will find Transcribed N chars (engine) instead. voxtype -v brings the text back.
  • Paste mode with no paste_keys in the config sent ctrl+v everywhere in 1.2.2. It now sends ctrl+shift+v to terminals on Hyprland, Sway and niri. Set paste_keys = "ctrl+v" for the old behaviour. A config with an explicit paste_keys, including the shift+insert in the packaged /etc/voxtype/config.toml, is unchanged.

v1.2.2: OSD Selection, Settings, and Output Fixes

Voxtype 1.2.2 improves the OSD and Settings panel, fixes transcript delivery and text cleanup bugs, and brings the Debian and RPM source packages up to date. It also adds setup instructions that you can install for a coding agent.

The OSD chooses an available frontend

The default OSD frontend is now auto. Voxtype uses Quickshell when it is available in a Wayland session, GTK4 when its libraries load, and the native OSD otherwise. An explicit gtk4, native or quickshell selection stays as configured. The new default also applies to existing configurations that omit the frontend key.

voxtype setup osd --status

This reports the configured frontend, what can run and why. On a compatible installation, Settings can offer to switch an explicit GTK4 or native selection to Quickshell. Choosing Not now is remembered. Picking a Quickshell style now switches to auto when supported, or explains why it cannot, instead of silently doing nothing.

Why use it: OSD styles work on installations where the previous GTK4 default prevented Quickshell from being selected.

Settings can switch every supported engine

The Settings panel can select installed models for every engine supported by the installed binary. Engine and model are validated and saved together. An unavailable engine or incompatible model is rejected without changing the configuration.

The restart banner also clears after restarts made outside the panel, and unset numeric values display their defaults instead of the minimum.

Why use it: You can switch engines from Settings without separately editing the engine and model from a terminal.

Output and audio failure handling

A closed output-helper pipe now returns an error instead of terminating the daemon with SIGPIPE. One-shot commands, status pipelines and transcription-worker output retain their existing behavior. Paste mode also falls back to the clipboard when pasting fails, rather than losing the transcript.

Device listing runs in a child process that is stopped if it exceeds its five-second deadline. Recording-device lookups have a five-second deadline per lookup. A streaming-to-batch fallback can require more than one lookup. These deadlines do not cancel native threads that are already stuck or guarantee normal daemon shutdown afterward.

Why use it: A failed output helper no longer kills the daemon, and an unresponsive sound server no longer leaves device enumeration waiting indefinitely.

A setup skill for coding agents

voxtype setup skill
voxtype setup skill --status

The installed skill tells a supported coding agent to inspect the running version, hardware, available engines and configuration before recommending changes. It covers model selection, output setup and older configuration defaults.

The command asks before replacing an edited skill. Without an interactive terminal, it leaves an edited copy alone unless replacement is explicitly forced. Package upgrades do not write into an agent's home-directory configuration.

Text cleanup and engine fixes

  • Cleanup edit notifications use supported formatting on Omarchy's newer notification panel instead of displaying raw font-color tags. Qt notification servers show deletions with strikethrough and insertions in bold; Pango servers retain their colors.
  • Punctuation and casing restoration capitalizes the correct letter instead of producing words such as "HEllo" or "fRiday".

  • The English Paraformer vocabulary no longer leaves YAML quotes around words such as "on", "yes" and "no", or around digits.
  • OpenVINO Whisper accepts language = "auto".
  • Soniox no longer reports a false unavailable-engine warning. Async transcription also deletes the uploaded audio file after use.
  • eitype handles text that starts with a hyphen.
  • ONNX builds and OSD helpers no longer emit an incorrect punctuation-casing warning while loading configuration.
  • Unknown or missing local Whisper model overrides are rejected before recording starts.
  • The user manual explains when sentence-break repair applies.

Desktop and packaging fixes

  • Notifications and the desktop launcher use Voxtype's microphone icon. Debian and RPM packages include the SVG and 128-pixel PNG icons.
  • The OSD discovers an available wayland-N socket when WAYLAND_DISPLAY is unset. CUDA and MIGraphX installations also find their packaged OSD helpers.
  • Package-install guidance handles symlinked package directories correctly on Linux and macOS. The macOS menu bar app no longer shows a Dock icon.

v1.2.1: Omarchy Settings Panel in the Packages, Smaller AUR Downloads

Voxtype 1.2.1 packages the Omarchy Settings panel, cuts the AUR voxtype-bin download to the variants your machine uses, and replaces "rebuild with --features" with install hints that fit how you installed voxtype. AUR voxtype-bin users move straight from 1.1.1 to 1.2.1, so the 1.2.0 changes arrive with this release too.

Omarchy Settings panel ships with voxtype

The deb, rpm and AUR packages now install the Settings panel at /usr/share/voxtype/omarchy-plugin/io.voxtype.settings. On Omarchy, the daemon links it into ~/.config/omarchy/plugins the first time it starts and enables it. The link points at the packaged copy, so the panel upgrades with voxtype, and the QML lives under a versioned path so Omarchy loads the new panel instead of a cached one.

voxtype setup omarchy            # link it now
voxtype setup omarchy --status   # linked, copy, linked-stale, ...
voxtype setup omarchy --replace  # swap a hand-installed copy for the packaged one

Voxtype never replaces a copy you installed with omarchy plugin add and never re-adds a panel you removed. If you installed the panel by hand before 1.2.1, the daemon tells you once, with a desktop notification, how to switch. Newer copies show a Switch banner with a Not now that is remembered. Opt out of the automatic link with:

config.toml
[omarchy]
link_settings_plugin = false

Why use it: the panel now arrives with the package and stays in step with the daemon it configures.

The panel tells you when it cannot run voxtype

If the panel cannot start the voxtype binary, it now says which binary and why, instead of showing settings from the last time it worked. Opening it from the tray's "Model update available" item re-reads everything. The tray's Settings item opens the panel when it is installed.

AUR voxtype-bin downloads only what you use

voxtype-bin picks variants when it builds: the CPU build for your processor, the baseline build, Vulkan, and whatever /usr/bin/voxtype runs now. That is about 170 MB instead of 914 MB. Add a variant with:

VOXTYPE_VARIANTS=onnx-cuda-13 yay -S --rebuild voxtype-bin

VOXTYPE_VARIANTS=all gets the old full set. If an upgrade cannot keep your backend (a clean-chroot build, for example), it says so loudly instead of switching you to the CPU quietly.

Install hints that match your install

voxtype setup model, setup onnx --enable and the daemon's engine warning used to tell package users to rebuild with --features. They now print the fix for your install: sudo voxtype setup onnx --enable, a VOXTYPE_VARIANTS rebuild, or a reinstall. On a Whisper package, the Parakeet section lists the GGUF Parakeet models that binary already runs.

GGUF upgrade offers only appear when the GGUF worker is installed, so a package without the worker no longer offers an upgrade that cannot work.

Still on an ONNX Parakeet or Cohere model? Voxtype offers the GGUF version of your model, and Parakeet users also get Parakeet Ultra. Offers show up in the tray menu, the Settings panel, the Waybar tooltip, the TUI and voxtype setup migrate. Nothing changes until you accept.

Accepting downloads the model, checks its sha256, switches engine and model in one validated write and restarts the daemon. If the download, switch or restart fails, config.toml is left exactly as it was.

voxtype setup migrate
voxtype setup migrate --accept parakeet-ultra
voxtype setup migrate --decline
voxtype setup migrate --cleanup

Why use it: GGUF models run on your GPU through Vulkan. On my RX 7800 XT, Parakeet Ultra transcribes 12 seconds of audio in 0.08 seconds.

Moving to GGUF is one way. Once you're on a GGUF model, Settings and the TUI stop listing that engine's ONNX models, and after your first successful dictation voxtype setup migrate --cleanup removes them. On my machine that freed 8.9 GB. The command line still accepts ONNX model names until 2.0.

voxtype setup --download --model cohere-transcribe-03-2026-Q4_K_M.gguf
config.toml
engine = "cohere"

v1.0.1: Cleaner audio, resumable downloads, and the first-week fixes

The first patch release after 1.0.0, covering everything reported in the days after it shipped. Four of these change what the audio pipeline actually produces, so this is worth installing even if none of the individual bugs bit you.

Band-limited resampling (#641)

The resampler that converts your microphone's rate to the 16kHz the models expect was doing linear interpolation with no anti-aliasing filter. Downsampling 48kHz to 16kHz folded every frequency above 8kHz back down into the speech band, so on the most common capture configuration there is, the model was handed audio dirtier than the microphone produced.

Two smaller faults came with it. The old function started fresh on every audio callback, so the fractional read position reset roughly a hundred times a second and left a discontinuity each time. And whatever sat in the buffer when you released the key was discarded, losing up to 21ms off the end of every recording.

Why it matters: better transcription accuracy on any machine whose microphone does not natively run at 16kHz, which is most of them, and your last word no longer gets clipped.

Media ducking fades

Ducking now ramps the volume down and back rather than jumping, and the fade runs without delaying the microphone. Configurable in milliseconds, and reachable from voxtype configure as well as the config file, the flag, and the environment variable.

config.toml
[audio]
duck_media = true
duck_media_volume_percent = 34
duck_media_fade_ms = 150      # 0 for the old instant behavior

Why use it: the old jump was jarring when dictating over music, and a slow fade that held up the microphone would have been worse. This does neither.

Model downloads resume (#645)

An interrupted download used to start over from zero, and a stalled one would hang indefinitely. Downloads now resume from where they stopped and abort when the transfer goes quiet. A partial file is never left at the final path, so a failed download cannot masquerade as a complete model.

Why it matters: the large ONNX models are a slow download on a domestic connection, and losing an hour of progress to a dropped connection was a genuine reason to give up.

Hotkeys that stay unstuck

Three separate faults, all of which left push-to-talk in a bad state. Plugging in a keyboard mid-press left the daemon believing the key was still held, so recording never stopped (#556). After voxtype typed through dotool, the listener kept the file descriptor of the tool's short-lived virtual keyboard; once the tool tore it down, the daemon spun a CPU core at 100 percent (#445). And dropped evdev events could desynchronize the pressed-key state.

Idle model eviction actually runs (#644)

The code to unload an idle model was written, correct, and had never once executed. It sat on a 500ms timer arm that a 100ms arm beside it starved: in a tokio::select! loop, an uncompleted timer future is recreated on every iteration, so a timer that never gets to complete never fires. Moved onto an arm that does.

Why it matters: long-running daemons on laptops now release model memory when idle, as the setting always claimed.

v0.7.3: Kaby Lake Vulkan SIGILL fix, RTX 5090 Blackwell support

A two-issue hotfix. The Vulkan binary regression came in via the move to GitHub-hosted CI runners; the CUDA 13 issue surfaced once RTX 5090 owners hit a transcription. Both fixes also harden the build pipeline so the same class of regression cannot ship again.

Vulkan binary works on Kaby Lake and older Intel CPUs (#393)

The v0.7.2 Vulkan binary failed with SIGILL on Intel CPUs that lack SSE4a, including the i7-7700HQ in the reporter's machine. The instruction came from RUSTFLAGS="-C target-cpu=native" in Dockerfile.vulkan. That flag was safe when the build host was a pre-AVX-512 Coffee Lake server, but the v0.7.2 pipeline moved onto GitHub's ubuntu-24.04 runner, which is AMD Zen 3 and has SSE4a. The compiler emitted insertq instructions, and Intel CPUs SIGILL on those.

Why use it: if you have a pre-Cascade-Lake Intel CPU (anything roughly 6th-gen or older) and the Vulkan backend, voxtype 0.7.2 daemons would not start. v0.7.3 boots cleanly on the same hardware.

The build now bounds Rust at target-cpu=haswell with -sse4a,-gfni,-avxvnni disables, matches the ggml CMake flags to the same floor, and adds a CI gate that fails the build on any AVX-512 EVEX, GFNI, AVX-VNNI, or SSE4a instruction in the AVX2 or Vulkan variants. The previous gate only checked zmm registers, so SSE4a slipped through unnoticed.

NVIDIA Blackwell (RTX 5090) works on the CUDA 13 binary (#386)

voxtype-onnx-cuda-13 shipped a CUDA execution provider with kernel coverage up to sm_90a (Hopper). Blackwell needs sm_120, so the RTX 5090 hit the EP and got cudaErrorNoKernelImageForDevice on every transcription. The upstream prebuilt ORT that the ort crate downloads does not include Blackwell, and the maintainer has marked Blackwell support as not planned.

Why use it: if you own an RTX 50-series card, voxtype 0.7.2's CUDA 13 binary would error on every transcription. v0.7.3 transcribes correctly on Blackwell.

Dockerfile.onnx-cuda-13 now pulls Microsoft's official ORT 1.24.4 cu13 prebuilt and links to it with ort/load-dynamic. The bundled CUDA EP covers sm_70 through sm_120a. The runtime ships alongside the binary at /usr/lib/voxtype/cuda-13/libonnxruntime.so.1.24.4 plus a libonnxruntime.so symlink, which is exactly where ort/load-dynamic looks first (relative to /proc/self/exe). No environment variable or LD_LIBRARY_PATH setup required.

Two build gates went in alongside the fix: a check that sm_120 markers are present in the bundled provider library, and a check that the binary did not silently fall back to static linking (which would have brought the old kernel coverage back).

voxtype-onnx-cuda-12 is unchanged. Blackwell requires CUDA 13 and driver 580+, so cuda-12 users are not affected.

Live transcription appears at the cursor as you speak, modifier keys no longer collide with typed output, and Waybar gets a dedicated streaming icon. Streaming requires toggle activation, not push-to-talk.

Streaming Dictation (experimental)

Parakeet streaming output types text incrementally while you dictate. Powered by the parakeet-rs cache-aware streaming pipeline, it works with parakeet-unified-en-0.6b (English) and runs on CPU, NVIDIA CUDA, or AMD MIGraphX.

Why use it: Words appear as you speak instead of after you release a key. Useful for long-form dictation, live notes, and interactive editing where waiting for batch transcription breaks flow.

Enable in the TUI Advanced section or in config:

config.toml
engine = "parakeet"

[parakeet]
model = "parakeet-unified-en-0.6b"
streaming = true

[hotkey]
mode = "toggle"

Streaming requires toggle activation. Push-to-talk does not work with streaming output: voxtype types characters at the cursor while you are still holding the key, and the synthetic key events from wtype or dotool clobber the held-key state tracker that Hyprland, Sway, and River use through libinput. After a few seconds the compositor decides the key was already released, so the actual release event never fires and the daemon stays in streaming mode.

Toggle activation sidesteps the issue entirely: no key is held during the session. The daemon emits a strong warning and auto-promotes push_to_talk to toggle for the running session when streaming is enabled, and the TUI rewrites the config to match.

Tunable parameters control the latency/accuracy tradeoff:

config.toml
[parakeet]
streaming_chunk_secs = 0.32         # how often partials are emitted
streaming_left_context_secs = 5.6   # history fed to each chunk
streaming_right_context_secs = 0.32 # lookahead per chunk

Modifier-Release Guard (#350)

When you bind voxtype to a chord like Super+Ctrl+X, the modifiers stay physically pressed while wtype types the transcription. On some compositors this caused the first typed letter to combine with the modifiers and fire an unrelated keybinding (closing windows, switching workspaces, opening menus).

The daemon now takes an EVIOCGKEY snapshot of keyboard state via evdev before typing output, and waits for any held modifier to be released before sending keystrokes. If the timeout is hit (default 750ms) the output falls back to clipboard so the transcription is not lost, and a desktop notification tells the user where the text went.

Why use it: No more "voxtype just closed my window" surprises after a long dictation. Works on Hyprland, Sway, River, and any compositor-agnostic setup where the user is in the input group.

config.toml
[output]
wait_for_modifier_release = true     # default when input group is available
modifier_release_timeout_ms = 750

Disabled automatically during streaming (your modifiers are still down because you are still actively dictating; the guard would never fire).

Notification Stacking (#345)

Voxtype's status notifications ("Recording...", "Transcribing...", "Transcribed", "Modifier key held...") now use the x-canonical-private-synchronous and transient libnotify hints, so a compositor with a notification daemon (mako, dunst, GNOME Shell, KDE) overwrites the previous Voxtype notification in place instead of stacking them in the notification history.

Why use it: The notification panel stays clean on GNOME/Ubuntu and similar setups instead of accumulating a long trail of "Recording..." / "Transcribing..." / "Transcribed" entries every time you dictate.

config.toml

v0.6.2: Echo Cancellation and Meeting Bug Fixes

Voxtype 0.6.2 adds neural echo cancellation for meeting mode, rewrites loopback audio capture, and fixes three meeting bugs. If you use meeting mode with loopback enabled, remote participants' audio no longer bleeds into your mic transcript.

GTCRN Echo Cancellation

When meeting mode captures both your microphone and system audio (for remote participant transcription), the remote audio bleeds through your speakers into your mic. Previous approaches using signal subtraction and energy gating didn't work because the mic and loopback streams aren't sample-aligned.

This release uses GTCRN (Group Temporal Convolutional Recurrent Network), a 48K-parameter speech enhancement model that processes mic audio frame-by-frame through STFT. It removes background noise and speaker bleed-through while preserving your voice. The model is 523 KB and runs at about 0.02x real-time on CPU, so processing 30 seconds of audio takes under 0.6 seconds.

Why use it: If you're in a video call and the other person's voice is showing up in your "You" segments, this fixes it. It's enabled by default when loopback capture is active.

The GTCRN model downloads automatically the first time you start a meeting. To disable it (if you already have system-level echo cancellation via PipeWire):

config.toml
[meeting.audio]
echo_cancel = "disabled"

Loopback capture has been rewritten to use parec (PulseAudio recording client) instead of cpal. PipeWire's monitor sources aren't visible through ALSA, which is what cpal uses. That's it. The command adds the module to your config, includes sensible CSS, and backs up your existing files first.

To remove it:

voxtype setup waybar --uninstall

Sometimes the best feature is removing a manual step.

December 27, 2025 Bug Fix

v0.4.2: GPU Acceleration on More Hardware

A user reported that GPU acceleration caused Voxtype to crash on their Ryzen 5950X. Same SIGILL error we fixed for CPU-only mode months ago.

The problem: whisper.cpp's Vulkan backend uses AVX-512 even when running on the GPU. My Zen 4 build machine has AVX-512. Their Zen 3 doesn't.

The fix was surgical: build the Vulkan binary with -mno-avx512f to prevent those instructions from being emitted. Now GPU acceleration works on:

  • Zen 3 (Ryzen 5000 series)
  • Zen 4 (Ryzen 7000/9000 series)
  • Intel 11th gen and newer
  • Most discrete GPUs with Vulkan support

If you tried Vulkan mode before and it crashed, v0.4.2 should work.

December 18, 2025 Bug Fix

Device Hotplug Fix (v0.3.2)

I locked my screen, came back, and Voxtype stopped working. The service was running, no errors in the logs, but pressing the hotkey did nothing.

Restarting the service fixed it. That's not a solution.

The problem: Voxtype uses evdev to capture keyboard input at the kernel level. When you lock your screen on Hyprland (and probably other Wayland compositors), the compositor releases its grip on input devices. When you unlock, the devices get re-added to /dev/input. But Voxtype was still holding file descriptors to the old device nodes—handles that now pointed to nothing.

The fix was inotify. Voxtype now watches /dev/input for device changes. When devices are added or removed, it validates its existing handles by checking /proc/self/fd/ to see if they still point to real devices. If not, it re-enumerates.

There's a 150ms debounce because USB devices don't enumerate instantly. And it clears modifier state on device changes so you don't end up with phantom stuck keys.

This is the kind of bug that only shows up in real usage. Unit tests don't lock your screen. I found it because I use the tool every day.

December 17, 2025 Bug Fix

Community Contributions & AVX-512 Fix (v0.3.1)

A user reported that Voxtype crashed immediately on their Ryzen 9 5950X. The error: SIGILL (illegal instruction). They couldn't even run voxtype --version.

I build on a Ryzen 9 9900X3D, which has AVX-512. Their Zen 3 chip doesn't. Simple enough—I was shipping binaries with instructions their CPU couldn't execute.

My first fix didn't work. I passed WHISPER_NO_AVX512=ON to the build, created separate AVX2 and AVX-512 binaries, and shipped a new release. The user tried it: still crashed.

They ran sha512sum on both binaries. Identical. I had shipped the same binary twice with different names.

The whisper.cpp version I'm using doesn't actually respect that flag. CMake's auto-detection finds AVX-512 on my build machine and enables it regardless. The flag gets passed, logged, and ignored.

The fix was to go lower: compiler flags. -mno-avx512f tells the compiler itself to never emit those instructions. Now the binaries are actually different.

This release also includes work from Mate Remias, who contributed three PRs in one day: paste output mode, on-demand model loading, and build script fixes.

December 15, 2025 New Feature

CJK Support (v0.3.0)

One of my first users of Voxtype reported that Korean speech-to-text wasn't working. They saw garbled output or a vague message to the effect of "korean isn't supported yet" (한국어는 지원되지 않습니다)

I spent a while debugging this. Whisper transcribed Korean fine—and indeed, something in the model was detecting Korean speech, so it couldn't be the model.

The problem was ydotool, which I had selected for injecting keyboard input based on speech transcription. It simulates keystrokes, which works great until you need actual Unicode support.

Here's the thing: I had looked at every other Linux voice-to-text tool I could find before I decided to build Voxtype. They all use ydotool or xdotool, which is why I selected it for my project.

None of them are going to handle Korean, Chinese, or Japanese properly either.

I built VoxType with a pluggable output module structure. So, solving this problem for my user was as simple as implementing wtype, which injects text directly instead of faking keystrokes. As far as I've been able to find, my project is the only speech-to-text tool using it for output right now.

I wasn't trying to corner the CJK market: I was just fixing a bug. But I'm genuinely not aware of another local voice-to-text tool on Linux that handles these languages correctly. If you know of one, tell me—I'd like to see how they solved it.

In the meantime, if you are a Linux user who wants speech to text, speaks Korean, Chinese, or Japanese, I think I've got the tool for you.

November 28, 2025 Launch

Initial Launch

I've spent years contributing to open source projects, but it's been a long time since I built and shipped a complete piece of software solo.

This Thanksgiving, I built voxtype, a push-to-talk voice transcription tool for Linux.

macOS and Windows have had solid voice typing for years, and even Google Docs has it built in. But on Linux, especially on Wayland, the options are limited. Most solutions are cloud-based, X11-only, or tied to specific desktop environments.

The few local options that exist are python-based. No shade to python, but requiring you to install pytorch, pytorchaudio, and a dozen other dependencies into a virtual environment, and remember to activate it every time you reboot is cumbersome. Vocalinux is pretty good but I got stuck in dependency hell trying to set it up, and I find it sluggish.

I wanted something simpler. Install a package, download a model, enable a service, done.

Voxtype is available from the AUR for Arch based systems, and as a .deb or .rpm package. Run a few commands post-install, enable the systemd user service and it just works every time you log in. No virtual environments, no dependency conflicts, no activation scripts (well, except systemd).

Hold a hotkey, speak, release, and your words appear at the cursor. It uses Whisper locally through whisper.cpp, so everything stays on your machine. Voxtype makes no network requests and uses no cloud transcription services.

The interesting technical challenge was making it work across all Wayland compositors. Since Wayland's security model blocks the usual approaches for global hotkeys and input simulation, voxtype works at the kernel level using evdev and uinput. This means it runs the same on Sway, Hyprland, or any other compositor.

It's a little "omakase" in that it's meant for Hyprland or Sway with Waybar. That's where you'll have the best experience. But it works on any compositor, and doesn't require Waybar.

It works great for me, which, to be candid, is enough for me. If you find it useful or have constructive/helpful feedback, that's a bonus.
